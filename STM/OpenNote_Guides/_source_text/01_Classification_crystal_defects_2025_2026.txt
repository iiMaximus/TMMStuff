Pure metals and metal alloys consist of atoms and are characterized by 
metallic bonding : the valence electrons of each atom, i.e. the electron 
available for the bond, are delocalized and shared among all the atoms.
Most of the elements in the Periodic Table are metals.
Examples of metal alloys are:
- Cu-Zn (brass) 
- Fe-C (steel) 
- Sn-Pb (an alloy used for soldering) 
Alloys are classified according to the majority element that is present in it.
The main classes of alloys are:
- iron-based alloys for structures (e.g.
reinforced concrete );
- copper-based alloys for piping, utensils, thermal conduction, electrical 
conduction, etc.; 
- aluminum-based alloys for 
lightweight structures and metal-matrix 
composites.

Dislocation are particularly significant in PURE METALS 
AND ALLOYS since they provide a mechanism for plastic 
deformation , which is the cumulative effect of slip of many 
dislocations .
Plastic deformation refers to IRREVERSIBLE 
DEFORMATION or CHANGE IN SHAPE that occurs when 
the force (stress) that cause it is removed. This is because 
the applied stress causes dislocation motion that, in 
turn, causes permanent deformation .
The plastic deformation is to be distinguished from elastic 
deformation , which is a TEMPORARY CHANGE IN 
SHAPE that occurs while a force or stress remains 
applied to a material. In elastic deformation, the shape 
change is a result of stretching of interatomic bonds , but 
NO MOTION OF DISLOCATIONS OCCURS .

HARDENING is obtained during deformation of a metallic material by 
increasing the number of dislocations .
Before deformation, the dislocation density is about 10 6 cm of dislocation 
line per cubic centimeter of material. When a stress greater than the yield 
strength is applied, dislocation begin to slip ( PLASTIC DEFORMATION 
occurs).
Eventually, a dislocation moving on its slip plane can encou nter obstacles 
that pin the ends of the dislocation line; as we continue to ap ply stress, the 
dislocation can create additional dislocations.
The dislocation density can increase to about 10 
12 cm of dislocation line 
per cubic centimeter of material: when we have too many dislo cations,
they interfere with their own motions .
The result is an increased strength but reduced ductility (i.e., the material 
is less prone to be wrought).

where crystalline order does not 
exist and the grains impinge on 
each other when the solidification is 
completed.

between grain surfaces with the
