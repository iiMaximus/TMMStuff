Ex. 3: Define the coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE), its units and list the CTE of polymers, 
metal, glasses and ceramics. Explain the list order. 
The relative expansion (also called strain) divided by the change in temperature is 
called the material's coefficient of linear thermal expansion (CTE),
stronger is the atomic bonding strength lower is the thermal expansion 
coefficient.
➢ Polymers: 100  x 10-6 K-1
➢ Metal: 15-30  x 10-6 K-1
➢ Glasses: 0,5-20  x 10-6 K-1
➢  Ceramics: 3-7  x 10-6 K-1
Ceramics → minimal expansion (very strong bonds, high melting temperature, low CTE . In opposite we have 
the behavior of polymer materials→weak bonds→high CTE

Conventionally compressive stress are considered negative while tensile ones as
positive. For this reason frequently, for thermally induced stresses, T is considered as
T0-T1 in order to obtain negative stress in case of elongation (compression of the
component heated with constrained ends) and positive in case of contraction (tensile
stress on the component cooled with constrained ends). If we consider T as T1-T0, as
before, in the stress calculation we should remember that in heating we have
elongation (positive) which results, in case of constrained ends, in a compressive stress
on the component, which is negative. In cooling we have a contraction of the material
(negative) which results, in case of constrained ends, in a tensile stress on the
component (positive).
Heating (T1>T0)
T=T1-T0 >0
>0 (elongation)
