Historical example 
In 1958, at the beginning of the US space project, an 
engineer proposed to use tungsten (W) with a purity of 
99.9% as the material for the exhaust nozzles of the 
engines.
The choice was based on a few points:
• tungsten has a melting temperature of 3410 °C, about 
400 °C above the temperature of the exhaust gases;
• tungsten is hard and can resist abrasion due to hot 
exhaust gases 
• tungsten was not too expensive ($ 25,000 per nozzle) as 
compared to advanced ceramics at that time


--- PAGE 30 ---
Three prototypes are produced and tests started.
The first nozzle only resisted a few seconds at about 3000 
°C and then melted 
The second and third nozzles melted even before reaching 
3000 °C
The residues of the two destroyed nozzles were analyzed;
the composition of the material was, in both cases, W =
98.5% and C = 1.5%
