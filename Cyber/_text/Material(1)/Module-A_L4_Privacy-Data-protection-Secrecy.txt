  Privacy as a Fundamental Right
    Privacy refers to the protection of an individual’s:
    ➢    Private sphere (personal life and autonomy)
    ➢    Communications (correspondence and interactions)
    ➢    Behavioural patterns (habits, preferences, profiling)
    ➢    Identity construction (freedom from unjustified surveillance)




                     Privacy protects the person, not just the data.

  How Privacy Protection is Operationalized
    Privacy protection typically requires:
    ➢ Data minimization
              • Collect only the data strictly necessary for a defined purpose
    ➢    Purpose limitation
              • Use data only for the purpose originally specified
    ➢    Informed consent
              • Ensure individuals understand and agree to how their data is used
    ➢    Control over access and disclosure
              • Restrict who can access or share personal information
    ➢    Limitations on retention
              • Retain data only for as long as necessary

                   Privacy requires mechanisms, not just intentions.

  Role of Technology
    ➢    Technology enables:
              •    large-scale data collection
              •    large-scale inference (i.e., the process of using massive datasets and automated
                   analysis to deduce additional information that was not explicitly provided)
              •    automated profiling (i.e., the automatic creation of a profile about a person by
                   analyzing their data, in order to evaluate or predict aspects such as behavior,
                   preferences, reliability, or risk)
              •    mass surveillance (i.e., the large-scale monitoring of people’s communications,
                   activities, or movements, often carried out systematically and affecting many
                   individuals at once, not just specific suspects)
    ➢    But, it also enables: encryption, access control, privacy-enhancing
         technologies (e.g., Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs))
    ➢  Technology must protect user privacy from attacks brought by
       technology itself

Secrecy – Definition
Secrecy concerns the protection of sensitive institutional or state
information.

➢ What it protects: sensitive institutional or state information
➢ Why it exists: to prevent harm that may result from
  disclosure
➢ Who defines the rules: institutional authority, mission needs,
  and public-interest requirements

    Unlike privacy, secrecy is centered on protecting strategic
          information, not the individual’s private sphere.

  The Privacy Paradox
    ➢    People say “I value privacy”, but in practice …
              • Accept default settings
              • Click “Agree” to access services quickly
              • Share more data than intended
    ➢ Why this happens
              •    Information asymmetry: users don’t know what is collected / inferred
              •    Bounded rationality: costs are immediate, privacy harms are
                   delayed/uncertain
              •    Dark patterns: interfaces steer choices (friction to refuse, nudges to
                   accept)
              •    ⟹ Consent becomes low-quality → weak signal of real preferences
                           Giving up personal data in exchange for free services
                             means giving up the right to self-determination!!

  European Regulatory Framework


  As of May 25, 2018, EU Regulation 2016/679, known as GDPR (General
  Data Protection Regulation) - on the protection of individuals with regard to
  the processing and free movement of personal data - is directly applicable
  in all Member States' legal systems.

      personal data across the EU
    ➢ Goal: harmonization + protection in the digital economy
    ➢ Key idea: accountability (organizations must prove
      compliance)
    ➢ Security becomes a design constraint, not an optional
      add-on

From Principles to Organizational Duties
GDPR principles become concrete duties in the design and
operation of information systems.
1.   Lawful basis and purpose definition: Personal data must be collected
     for a clear and legitimate purpose
2.   Data minimization and retention limits: Collect only what is necessary
     and keep it only as long as needed
3.   Access control and protection measures: Organizations must restrict
     access and secure data in storage and transit
4.   Transparency and data-subject rights: Users must be informed and
     able to access, correct, or erase their data where applicable
5.   Documentation and incident readiness: Compliance must be
     demonstrable, and breaches must be detected, assessed, and managed

  Data breach
    ➢    Security breach that accidentally or unlawfully results in the
         destruction, loss, modification, unauthorized disclosure of or
         access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise
         processed.
                                                              [ISO/IEC 27040:2015]
    Data breaches can affect:
    ➢ Confidentiality (unauthorized disclosure/access): e.g., leaked
      database
    ➢ Integrity (unauthorized modification): e.g., tampered medical
      record
    ➢ Availability (loss/destruction/unavailability): e.g., ransomware
      encryption

  Mechanisms for Information Protection
    ➢ Access Control
      Restricts who can access which data and under what conditions.
    ➢ Encryption
      Protects data by transforming it into unreadable form without a
      cryptographic key.
    ➢ Anonymization
      Reduces identifiability of individuals in datasets (i.e., an
      organized collection of related data, usually stored in a structured
      way so it can be searched, analyzed, or processed).
    ➢ Pseudonymization
      Replaces identifiers with reversible tokens to reduce risk.

  Encryption
    ➢ Encryption transforms data into ciphertext using a cryptographic
      key.
    ➢ Used for:
        • data at rest (disks, databases, or backups)
        • data in transit (network communication)
    ➢ Security objective:
        • protect confidentiality even if storage or transmission
           channels are compromised.
    ➢ Encryption protects data, but does not regulate how it is used
      once decrypted
        • encryption ≠ privacy

  Logging and Accountability
    ➢    Logging is often realized through event records automatically
         generated and stored by systems, most commonly:
              • stored as log files on servers, applications, databases, and operating
                systems
              • structured as audit trails for user and administrator actions
              • collected in centralized logging platforms from multiple sources
    ➢ Logging records events related to: access to data, data
      modification, system activity
    ➢ It enables traceability, allows to detect anomalies, support
      incident investigation
              • GDPR compliance and breach detection support
    ➢    Logging is a detective control, not a preventive control.

  Anonymization
    ➢    Technique applied to personal data in such a way that the
         individuals concerned can no longer be identified in any way.

    Key characteristics:
    • Removes or alters direct identifiers
    • Reduces linkability
    • Requires contextual risk assessment

    ➢ The effectiveness of anonymization also depends on the
      surrounding environment: what other data exist, what auxiliary
      information is available, what kinds of correlation are possible
Cybersecurity and National Defense (Grandi Sfide - Digital)

A.A. 2025 - 2026
  Anonymization
    ➢    Goal: Eliminate the correlation between personal data and the
         individual they refer to, making it impossible to identify that
         person.

    ➢    Consequence: By the time personal data referring to an
         individual has been properly anonymized, it should be impossible
         to reverse the process.

    ➢    GDPR: If the anonymization has been properly implemented, the
         data are no longer classified as personal data and therefore do
         not fall under the application dimension of the GDPR.

  Pseudonymization
    ➢    The processing of personal data in such a manner that the
         personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data
         subject without the use of additional information, provided
         that such additional information is kept separately and is subject
         to technical and organisational measures to ensure that the
         personal data are not attributed to an identified or identifiable
         natural person.

  Pseudonymization
    ➢ Pseudonymization replaces identifying fields with artificial
      identifiers (tokens).
    ➢ Characteristics:
         • Reversible with additional information
         • Data remain personal data
         • Reduces impact of breaches (i.e., not every actor who sees
            the pseudonymized dataset can immediately identify the
            person behind it)
         • Supports privacy-by-design strategies
    ➢ Pseudonymization reduces risk but does not eliminate
      regulatory obligations.

  Secrecy as Institutional Protection
    ➢ Secrecy protects information whose disclosure would harm
      collective interests.
    ➢ It typically concerns:
              •    National security
              •    Strategic infrastructure
              •    Military operations
              •    Intelligence activities
              •    or other high-impact state functions
    ➢    Unlike privacy, secrecy protects:
              • Institutions, missions, and the State
              • Not individual personal rights
               Secrecy is a governance mechanism for managing high-
                                                    impact risk.

  Classification Levels in Italy
    The Italian law provides for four classifications of secrecy, defined
    as National, to which correspond increasing levels of information
    protection:
       ○ RISERVATO (R), slight damage to the interests of the Republic;

       ○ RISERVATISSIMO (RR), damage to the interests of the

         Republic;
       ○ SEGRETO (S), serious damage to the interests of the Republic;

       ○ SEGRETISSIMO (SS), exceptionally serious damage to the

         interests of the Republic.
                                                            [Legge 3 agosto 2007, n. 124
                    Decreto del Presidente del Consiglio dei Ministri 12 giugno 2009, n. 7]

Need-to-Know Principle
Even with authorization or clearance, access should be limited to
information strictly necessary for a specific role or mission.

➢ Clearance is not unlimited access
➢ Distribution is constrained by operational necessity
➢ The goal is to reduce exposure and limit damage if compromise
  occurs

In secrecy regimes, authorization and need-to-know work together
to control dissemination.

  Secrecy and National Defense
    Secrecy supports national defense by:
    ➢ Protecting strategic capabilities
              • if an adversary knows too much about military systems, plans,
                vulnerabilities, logistics, the effectiveness of those capabilities may be
                reduced
    ➢    Preventing adversarial intelligence gathering
              • information denied to the adversary is often as important as information
                possessed by one’s own side
    ➢    Reducing systemic vulnerability
              • large-scale disclosure may expose exploitable weaknesses
    ➢ However: Excessive secrecy reduce transparency
    ➢ Democratic systems require balance
