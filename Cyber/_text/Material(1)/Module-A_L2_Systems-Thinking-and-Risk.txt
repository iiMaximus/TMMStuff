    ➢    This definition bridges general threats to cyber threats.

    ➢    The nature and peculiarities of the concepts of danger and threat
         strongly depend on the target context and application domain.

              ➢    In a civilian context, relevant threats could include
                    •  Location privacy violations
                    •  Manipulation of navigation data
              ➢    In a military context, threats to be considered could be
                    •  Satellite signal spoofing by an adversary
                    •  Strategic denial of navigation signals

    ➢    Security requires an asset view: no assets → no meaningful

             • assets to which security applies

  Cybersecurity
    ➢    Cybersecurity deals with intentional, intelligent, and adaptive
         threats acting through cyberspace.

    ➢ What makes cybersecurity different is the environment in
      which these threats operate.
    ➢ In cyberspace:
              • attacks can be executed remotely,
              • they can spread very quickly across interconnected systems,
              • and a single vulnerability may affect thousands or millions of systems
                simultaneously.

  The concept of Failure
    ➢ In general, failure is defined as the impossibility to meet a
      specific requirement, objective, or performance measure.
    ➢ Specifically, a failure is usually defined in terms of:
              ➢    behaviour exhibited by the system
              ➢    interactions between the elements that compose it
              ➢    produced results


    ➢    Example: an infusion pump delivers the wrong dosage due to a
         sensor fault.

  Threat Chain – Different perceptions
    ➢ The so-called threat chain describes how failures emerge (not
      how attacks are executed), i.e., the set of steps that leads to
      failures.
    ➢ The threat chain is perceived differently within different
      communities.
    ➢ In the sequel, we briefly analyze how the threat chain is
      perceived by:
          ➢ the “Safety community”

          ➢ the “Software community”

          ➢ the “Security community”

    ➢    Braking system of a car equipped with ABS (Anti-lock Braking

  The “Software community”
    It usually resort to other definitions:
    ➢ Error: a human action that produces an incorrect result.
    ➢ Bug: an error or a fault that causes a failure (used informally in
        place of fault).
    ➢ Fault: an incorrect step, process, or data definition in a computer
        program.
    ➢ Failure: the inability of software to perform its required functions
        within specified performance requirements. A failure occurs at
        runtime, when the system executes the faulty code.

                                      [IEEE Standard Glossary of Software Engineering Terminology]

  Dependability Elements
    Dependability can be broken down into three elements:
    ➢ Attributes: the properties that characterize a dependable
      system.
    ➢ Threats: the events or conditions that can affect the
      dependability of a system.
    ➢ Means: mechanisms and techniques to increase a system’s
      dependability.

    ➢    Resilience is the ability of a system to:
           • prepare for adverse events
           • absorb disruption while maintaining essential functions
           • recover within mission-acceptable time
           • adapt to evolving threats and conditions
