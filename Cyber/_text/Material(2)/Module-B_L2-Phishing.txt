    ➢    internet fraud in which the attacker attempts to trick the victim

            ➢ provide personal information, such as login credentials, bank

                account and credit card details​
            ➢ click on malicious links

            ➢ open attachments that contain malware

   Phishing – How It Is Realized
    ➢ typically by sending e-mails messages, more or less targeted,
      that mimic in graphics and settings, bank or postal sites that
      require to send personal data
    ➢ scammers often craft email subject lines to trigger strong
      emotions or create urgency
    ➢ more sophisticated ones make their messages seem legitimate
      by using subjects the impersonated sender would realistically
      use, such as “Problem with your order” or “Your invoice is
      attached.”

A.Y. 2025 - 2026
   Spear Phishing
    ➢ it is a highly targeted form of phishing aimed at a specific
      individual, often someone with access to sensitive information
      or special authority, like a finance manager who can transfer
      company funds
    ➢ using insights, attackers create messages filled with personal
      details that make them appear authentic
    ➢ they might pretend to be the target’s boss and send a message
      like: “I know you’re heading on vacation tonight, but could you
      take care of this invoice before the end of the day?”

   Whaling
    ➢ spear phishing but adopted for the highest-value targets
    ➢ whaling focuses on high-ranking individuals such as C-level
      executives (CEOs, CFOs, COOs), senior leaders, political
      figures, and others with the authority to approve large payments,
      wire transfers, or the release of sensitive information without
      needing additional approval
    ➢ these individuals are called “whales”, having access to
      significantly more money than the average person

   Business Email Compromise (BEC)
    ➢ BEC is a type of spear-phishing attack that aims to trick
      organizations into handing over money or sensitive information
    ➢ a cybercriminal or group of attackers sends emails to employees
      that appear to come from a colleague, vendor, partner, customer,
      or another trusted contact
    ➢ these messages are designed to deceive recipients into paying
      fake invoices, transferring funds to fraudulent accounts, or
      revealing sensitive information such as customer data,
      intellectual property, or financial records.
    ➢ scammers can gain unauthorized access to the sender’s email
      account, making their fraudulent messages appear more
      authentic.

   Other Forms of Phishing (I)
    ➢    vishing
            ➢ scammers frequently use caller ID spoofing to make their

               calls look like they’re coming from trusted organizations or
               local numbers
            ➢ these vishing calls often alarm recipients with claims of

               credit card issues, unpaid bills, or legal trouble, prompting
               them to share sensitive information or send money to “fix”
               the problem

A.Y. 2025 - 2026
   Other Forms of Phishing (II)
    ➢ smishing
         ➢ SMS phishing (smishing) relies on fraudulent text messages

            to deceive victims
         ➢ scammers often impersonate mobile carriers, sending

            messages that promise a “free gift” or prompt users to
            update their credit card details.
    ➢ via social media
         ➢ scammers may impersonate others to trick victims into

            sharing login credentials, enabling account takeover
         ➢ the risk is greater for people who reuse passwords across

            multiple accounts.

   Microsoft’s DDE (I)
    ➢ Microsoft’s Dynamic Data Exchange (DDE) is a protocol
      designed to allow the transportation of data between MS Office
      applications
    ➢ It was introduced as early as Windows 2.0 back in 1987 and
      provides what Microsoft once considered core functionality to the
      Office suite of tools
    ➢ Although DDE has now been superseded by the Object Linking
      and Embedding (OLE) toolkit, DDE is still supported by Office
      applications for backwards-compatibility

    ➢ while that is certainly a benefit for legitimate users and uses of

    ➢ DDE allows the execution of embedded code once a victim

   Email – Cyber Hygiene
    ➢ remember that no serious organization will ever ask you to
      submit passwords or click on a link included in an email
    ➢ adopt policies that prevent the execution of macros or VBA
      (Visual Basic for Applications) code present in Office source files
      (Word, Excel, Powerpoint)
    ➢ do not email Office source files
    ➢ install only “quality” PDF readers
    ➢ if you really must open an attachment to an Email, do so within
      Virtual Machines or Sandboxes, only

   Virtual Machines
    ➢ a program that, through a virtualization process, creates a virtual
      environment that emulates the behaviour of a physical machine
      (PC, client or server) thanks to the allocation of hardware
      resources (portions of disk, RAM and computing resources) and
      in which it is possible to run applications as if they were
      interacting with that machine
    ➢ even if the Operating System running on the virtual machine gets
      compromised, the Operating System of the hosting machine
      would be neither affected nor damaged


Cybersecurity and National Defence (Grandi Sfide - Digital)

A.Y. 2025 - 2026
   Sandbox
    ➢ virtual environment, “isolated” from the rest of the computer, that
      allows you to run non-certified applications, download files, and
      visit suspicious websites without exposing the system on which
      the sandbox is running to risks and threats
    ➢ some examples:
          ➢ Windows Sandbox (WSB)

          ➢ Linux sandboxing (namespaces, chroot)

             ➢ App sandboxing

   The XZ Backdoor Case
    ➢ the XZ package is used for data compression
    ➢ it is widely used
          ➢ adopted in the OpenSSH remote access software

    ➢ a contributor called “Jia Tan” was able to obtain maintainer-level
      access to the project
    ➢ exploiting the privilege Tan introduces a backdoor in the code
      that allowed to run arbitrary code on the victim machine when the
      XZ was loaded into an OpenSSH server
    ➢ it has been discovered before getting into release distributions
