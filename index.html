Professor-style transformation reading

Heat treatment map

Read the path, keep the remaining austenite, then name the final products.

The exam trick is not artistic graph reading. It is bookkeeping: start with austenite, mark every transformation region the path enters, estimate what fraction transforms, and carry the rest forward until martensite or residual austenite is decided.

Step 1

Read the map before reading the path.

    Professor shortcut

    A / AR Austenite and residual austenite

    A is the parent phase before transformation. AR is the austenite still left at room temperature after the path did not finish transforming it.

    F / P Ferrite and pearlite

    Ferrite may form first in hypo-eutectoid steels. Pearlite is ferrite plus cementite lamellae; coarse at higher temperature, finer and harder lower down.

    BU / BL Upper and lower bainite

    Upper bainite forms in the higher bainite range. Lower bainite forms closer to Ms, is finer, and is often grouped with martensitic/tempered-martensite labels in exam sketches.

    M / TM Martensite and tempered martensite

    Martensite starts below Ms without diffusion. TM is the tempered martensite label used in several exam diagrams after martensite has been heat treated.

    Exam answer recipe

    For percentage questions, do the same bookkeeping every time.

    Based on Lecture 9 Steel Heat Treatment and the converted TTT graph questions from the past exams.

    1. Begin with 100% austenite after austenitizing.
    2. At each hold or cooling segment, write the product whose start curve is crossed.
    3. If the path stops inside a field, write product + remaining A, not only the product.
    4. Carry the untransformed austenite to the next lower region.
    5. Below Ms, convert only the reached martensite fraction; what remains at room temperature is AR.
    6. If the exam says neglect phases below 5%, drop tiny products from the final mixture.
